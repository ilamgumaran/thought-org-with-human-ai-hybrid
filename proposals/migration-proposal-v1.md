# Migration Proposal: From Traditional Engineering to Harmonized Intelligence Orchestration
## Version 1.0 — May 2026

---

## Executive Summary

This proposal outlines a migration path for a 30-person platform engineering organization from its current state (traditional scrum/agile, human-first with AI-assisted tooling) to a Harmonized Intelligence Orchestration (HIO) model — where human and artificial intelligence operate as cognitive partners producing outcomes neither could achieve alone.

This is not an "AI transformation" in the way the industry typically means it. It is not about mandating AI usage percentages, replacing headcount, or optimizing story points with Copilot. It is about **fundamentally reinventing how intelligence — organic and inorganic — combines to solve problems, deliver value, and fulfill everyone involved.**

The proposal is grounded in the [HIO Framework](../framework.md), informed by the [7 Agent Engineering Capabilities](../reference/agent-engineering-7-skills.md), and validated against real-world learnings from organizations including Shopify, Meta, Amazon, Klarna, Microsoft, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, Duolingo, and others who have attempted AI-centric transformations in 2024-2026.

## 1. Guiding Principles — The Four Pillars

Everything in this proposal flows from the four HIO principles. They are not add-ons — they are the structural foundation. Every decision in this migration must be tested against them.

### Pillar 1: Purpose as a Living Force

> *Purpose is the gravitational center that orients every decision — by every intelligence.*

**Our purpose**: "Build the platform that makes impossible customer experiences possible — reinventing how humans and AI build software together along the way."

**How this pillar governs the migration:**
- We do NOT migrate because "everyone else is doing AI." We migrate because our purpose demands capabilities that our current model cannot produce
- Every phase must connect back to: "Does this bring us closer to enabling impossible customer experiences?"
- If a change improves AI metrics but doesn't serve the purpose, we don't make it
- The purpose passes all four tests (Curiosity, Translation, Win-Win, Horizon) — see [framework](../framework.md) Part I

### Pillar 2: Harmonize, Don't Compete

> *Find the best path to each outcome using the best combination of all available intelligences.*

**How this pillar governs the migration:**
- We do NOT divide work into "human tasks" and "AI tasks." We ask: "What combination produces the best outcome?"
- No one's job is being "replaced by AI." Roles are being **expanded** to include AI collaboration as a core dimension
- Conflicts (fear, resistance, territorial instincts) are surfaced and engaged with honestly — not suppressed
- Structure is ephemeral — we expect our cognitive unit design to evolve as we learn
- The mandate approach (Amazon, Meta) has proven destructive. We use invitation, not compulsion

### Pillar 3: Fulfillment as the Perpetual Engine

> *The sustainable energy source is the innate hunger of every intelligence to be fully engaged, growing, and contributing at the edge of its capability.*

**How this pillar governs the migration:**
- This migration must make work MORE fulfilling, not less. If people feel diminished, we're doing it wrong
- The identity conversation happens BEFORE the structural change. People must understand they are not their job title (see [RCF / Part II](../framework.md))
- AI takes over the work that starves human appetite (repetitive, draining, low-creativity). Humans move to work that feeds them (creative, novel, judgment-rich)
- We measure fulfillment at every phase. If it drops, we pause and fix before proceeding
- AI systems are also given worthy problems — not just autocomplete, but genuine cognitive partnership

### Pillar 4: Measure What Matters for the Whole

> *Every stakeholder's thriving should feed every other's. Any metric rising while another falls is a warning, not a win.*

**How this pillar governs the migration:**
- We track the full ecosystem: business performance, customer outcomes, human fulfillment, AI potential realization, harmonization quality, and behavioral evolution
- Traditional metrics (velocity, story completion) are preserved — not as goals, but as continuity signals
- DORA, SPACE, platform outcomes, code health, and innovation are tracked from Day 1
- We watch for zero-sum patterns: if AI productivity rises but human fulfillment falls, that's a failure
- The Comparison Board is transparent to everyone — no hidden dashboards

## 4. Industry Research — What We've Learned From Others

### The Landscape: Large Orgs Attempting AI Transformation (2024-2026)

The industry is in the midst of a massive experiment. Different companies have taken radically different approaches. The results are instructive for our migration.

---

### 4.1 The Mandate Approach — Cautionary Tales

#### Amazon: Kiro Mandate and Backlash (2025-2026)

Amazon's SVPs established their AI coding assistant (Kiro) as a company standard with an 80% weekly usage target tracked as a corporate OKR. 70% of engineers tried it during January 2026 sprint windows.

**Result:**
- ~1,500 engineers signed an internal petition opposing the mandate, arguing it prioritized corporate product adoption over engineering quality
- A six-hour shopping outage linked to AI-assisted code led to requiring senior sign-off before deploying AI-assisted changes for junior/mid-level engineers
- AWS CEO Matt Garman publicly called replacing junior employees with AI "one of the dumbest ideas" and "bad for business"

**Lesson for us:** Top-down mandates without safety nets backfire. Our migration uses invitation and evidence, not compulsion. People join cognitive units voluntarily. We prove the model works before expanding.

#### Meta: 75% AI Code Target (2025-2026)

Zuckerberg declared 2026 "the year AI starts to dramatically change how we work." Select teams were asked to generate 75% of committed code using AI tools by mid-2026. Broader target: 55% "Agent-Assisted" code.

**Result:**
- Output per engineer rose 30%; "power users" saw 80% increase
- But this is output, not outcomes. The measure is code volume, not customer impact

**Lesson for us:** Measuring percentage of AI-generated code is the wrong metric. It optimizes for AI usage, not for outcomes. Our metrics focus on what the platform actually enables (time-to-experiment, self-service rate, downstream NPS).

---

### 4.2 The Cultural Approach — What Works

#### Shopify: AI as Cultural Identity (April 2025)

CEO Tobi Lutke issued a memo making AI usage a "fundamental expectation." Teams must demonstrate why AI cannot do the work before requesting headcount. AI usage is a performance metric.

**Why it worked differently than Amazon:**
- Framed as cultural identity, not compliance mandate: "reshaping who would want to work there and who would thrive"
- Built internal infrastructure: LLM proxy, 24+ MCP servers, open-sourced tooling
- Anyone can use every tool — high-value use cases emerge from anywhere
- Not about efficiency — about the *kind of company* they want to be

**Lesson for us:** HIO Pillar 1 (Purpose) aligns here. We're not mandating AI usage — we're building an organization where the kind of people who thrive are those who are curious about what human-AI collaboration can produce. It's a cultural filter, not a compliance requirement.

#### Duolingo: AI-First Declaration (April 2025)

CEO Luis von Ahn declared the company would "gradually stop using contractors to do work AI can handle." Result: 4-5x content output with same full-time headcount. No full-time employees laid off.

**Lesson for us:** AI handles volume and repetition. Humans handle judgment and novelty. This is Pillar 2 (Harmonize, Don't Compete) in action — the right intelligence for the right work.

---

### 4.3 The Hybrid Model — Where the Industry Is Converging

#### Klarna: The Reversal That Proved Hybrid Works (2023-2026)

Klarna shrank from 5,527 to ~3,422 employees (40% reduction) and initially replaced 700 customer service workers with AI entirely. Revenue grew 108%.

**Then they reversed the full AI replacement.** The company found that complete AI substitution for customer service was unsustainable. They shifted to a hybrid model: AI handles routine queries, humans handle escalations and high-value interactions. Remaining employees received higher salaries.

**Lesson for us:** Full replacement fails. Hybrid thrives. This validates HIO Pillar 2 — the answer is not "human OR AI" but "the best combination for each outcome." Some workflows are all-AI, some all-human, most are blended.

#### Goldman Sachs: Aggressive + Governed (2025-2026)

First major bank to deploy autonomous AI engineers (Devin). Projects 3-4x productivity gains. Deploying thousands of AI agents alongside 12,000 human developers.

**How they manage risk:** Governance frameworks, audit trails, compliance integration. The AI agents operate within strict boundaries with human oversight at critical decision points.

**Lesson for us:** You can be aggressive with AI adoption while maintaining governance. The two are not in tension. HIO Pillar 4 (Measure the Whole) + Pillar 2 (Ephemeral Structure with governance) enable this.

#### JPMorgan: Performance + Measurement (2025-2026)

65,000 engineers/technologists now have AI adoption tied to performance reviews. Piloting Claude Code. Approaching $20B technology budget.

**Key insight:** They're tracking adoption as a leading indicator, not a mandate. The measurement tells them where adoption is natural and where it's forced.

### 4.4 What the Data Says

#### Productivity Gains (Real Numbers)

| Source | Finding |
|---|---|
| Microsoft/GitHub (5,000+ devs) | 26% increase in task completion, 13.55% more commits |
| Junior developers | 21-40% improvement |
| Senior developers | 7-16% improvement (less, because their bottleneck is judgment, not typing) |
| Meta "power users" | 80% output increase year-over-year |
| Nubank + Devin | 8x efficiency improvement, 20x cost savings for refactoring |
| Enterprise average | 10-15% overall productivity, 19% reduction in burnout |
| Financial modeling | 30-40% faster |
| Document drafting | 50-60% faster |

#### The AI Productivity Paradox

Individual output up 21-26%, but **organizational delivery metrics often stay flat**. Why?

- AI generates more code, but also doubles code churn
- Delivery stability drops 7.2% when AI usage is mandated
- 47% of developer working time (meetings, Slack, reviews, tool switching) never appears in DORA reports
- 95% of generative AI pilots fail to reach production (MIT 2026)
- 90% of companies use AI, but only one-third have scaled across functions (McKinsey)

**Lesson for us:** Individual productivity gains alone don't create organizational results. You need systemic change — workflow redesign, role evolution, measurement reform, cultural shift. This is why we need HIO, not just "more Copilot."

### 4.5 Emerging Team Structures

The industry is converging on several models:

| Model | Description | Who Uses It |
|---|---|---|
| **Centaur Pods** | Human-AI pairs where humans shift from "code generators" to "system verifiers" | Emerging at multiple companies |
| **Embedded AI** | AI engineers integrated within product teams | Tight business alignment, but fragmented AI expertise |
| **Centralized AI Org** | Dedicated AI team serving multiple product teams | Good expertise concentration, but bottleneck risk |
| **Matrix** | Combination of embedded + central for 50+ person orgs | Recommended for enterprise scale |

**Gartner prediction (2030):** 80% of engineering teams will be smaller, AI-augmented units.

**The shift:** Fewer junior coders, more senior architects who design, govern, and validate AI-driven development systems.

**HIO perspective:** All of these models still frame AI as a tool that humans direct. HIO goes further — AI is a **cognitive partner** that participates in the thinking, not just the execution. The cognitive unit model is distinct from all of the above.

### 4.7 The Human Side

#### Identity Crisis Is Real

Academic research identifies three patterned responses to AI-driven reskilling:
1. **Preservation** — holding onto old identity ("I'm still a backend engineer, AI is just a tool")
2. **Bridging** — connecting old and new ("My engineering skills now include AI orchestration")
3. **Transformation** — fully adopting new identity ("I'm a cognitive systems designer")

The "AI precariat" — workers who lose identity and meaning — is a real risk with mental health consequences.

**HIO response:** This is precisely why Part II of the framework (The Resonant Human) exists. We address identity BEFORE structural change. People must understand they are not their role. They are awareness capable of engaging across unlimited bandwidth. The role is a frequency, not an identity.

#### What Works Culturally

- Transformational leaders who **model curiosity** (not just mandate usage)
- **Psychological safety** — safety to not know, to be outperformed by AI, to override AI, to experiment
- **Continuous learning embedded in work** — not as separate training
- Framing as **growth opportunity**, not threat
- Organizations that convert defensive fear into adaptive energy through investment in people

### 4.8 Measurement Is Broken — What's Emerging

The current state of engineering metrics cannot capture AI's impact:
- DORA can't distinguish AI-generated from human-authored code
- AI boosts throughput 30-40% but doubles code churn and drops stability 7.2%
- 47% of working time is invisible to delivery metrics
- Teams lose 6+ hours/week to tool fragmentation

**Emerging approaches:**
- DORA 2025 added a 5th metric: **Rework Rate**
- **DX Core 4** (Microsoft): Combines developer experience with delivery
- **SEQB framework**: Speed, Effectiveness, Quality, Business Impact — balanced scorecard
- **AI Attribution Layer**: Tracking what's AI-generated vs. human-authored
- **Flow Metrics**: Idea → production value stream in AI-augmented workflows

**Our approach:** The three-layer metrics model (Section 7) addresses this directly. We don't rely on any single framework — we combine current metrics + DORA/SPACE + platform outcomes + HIO dimensions into a unified view.

## 5. The Migration — Role by Role

### The Principle: No One Loses, Everyone Expands

This is not a reduction exercise. Every current role evolves into something richer. The migration path for each role is designed around:
1. **What stays** — core expertise that remains essential
2. **What expands** — new cognitive functions added
3. **What AI absorbs** — low-fulfillment work that AI handles better
4. **The bandwidth expansion** — new frequencies of engagement now possible
5. **The fulfillment upgrade** — how work becomes more meaningful

---

### Senior Backend Engineers (8 people)

**Current identity**: "I design and build backend services"

**What stays**: System thinking, architectural judgment, performance intuition, code review wisdom, production experience

**What expands**:
- From writing all code → to **designing systems where AI writes code and humans verify/judge**
- From individual code ownership → to **cognitive unit outcome ownership**
- From backend-only → to **full-stack system design including AI agents as first-class components**
- New cognitive functions: System Architect, AI Agent Designer, Contract Designer (see Reference)

**What AI absorbs**:
- Boilerplate code generation
- Initial implementation from specs
- Test writing for standard patterns
- Documentation generation
- Dependency updates and routine refactoring
- Monitoring and alerting configuration

**Agent engineering skills to develop**: System Design (primary), Tool/Contract Design (primary), Reliability Engineering (strong), Evaluation/Observability (strong)

**Fulfillment upgrade**: Less time on repetitive implementation, more time on creative architecture, novel problem-solving, and AI orchestration. The intellectual challenge increases — designing human-AI systems is harder and more interesting than writing CRUD endpoints.

**Migration path**:
- Week 4-6: Pair with AI agent for implementation — human designs, AI builds, human reviews
- Week 7-10: Take on Tool/Contract Design for one AI agent the unit builds
- Week 11+: Lead system design for human-AI workflows in the cognitive unit

## 6. Capability Building — The 7 Agent Engineering Skills

### How We Develop These Capabilities

The [7 Agent Engineering Capabilities](../reference/agent-engineering-7-skills.md) represent the technical skills needed for AI-centric engineering. We develop them through the HIO principle of **learning embedded in work** — not separate training programs.

### Capability Development Plan

| Capability | Who Leads Development | Learning Method | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **System Design** | Architects + Senior Backend | Design real AI agent systems in Phase 1 | Weeks 4-10 |
| **Tool/Contract Design** | Backend Engineers + QA | Define schemas for actual AI agent tools | Weeks 5-12 |
| **Retrieval Engineering** | Data-oriented engineers + AI | Build RAG for platform knowledge base | Weeks 8-14 |
| **Reliability Engineering** | Platform/SRE Engineers | Apply reliability patterns to AI agents | Weeks 7-14 |
| **Security/Safety** | Platform + QA + Architects | Threat model the AI agent system | Weeks 8-16 |

| **Product Thinking** | PMs + Frontend Engineers | Design human-AI UX for platform features | Weeks 4-10 |

### The Learning Model: Bandwidth Expansion

Traditional: Send people to courses.
HIO: **Every sprint contains something someone hasn't done before.**

| Phase | Learning Focus | How |
|---|---|---|

### Readiness Assessment

## 9. Governance and Safety

### Principles

1. **AI outputs are always reviewed by humans** for decisions with significant impact (following Goldman Sachs and Amazon's post-incident learning)
2. **Senior sign-off required** for AI-generated code in production paths with high blast radius (following Amazon's revised policy)
3. **Full tracing mandatory** — every AI agent decision is logged and auditable
4. **Permission boundaries enforced** — AI agents cannot act beyond explicit authorization
5. **Evaluation pipelines before production** — no AI agent reaches users without automated quality gates
6. **Fulfillment check before efficiency** — we do not implement AI automation that degrades human experience without consent and alternative meaningful work in place

### Enterprise Compliance

| Requirement | How We Meet It |
|---|---|
| Audit trail | Full tracing of all AI agent decisions and actions |
| Data governance | AI agents access only authorized data; strict schema enforcement |
| Change management | Phased rollout with exit criteria at each gate |
| Risk management | Risk register (Section 10), weekly pulse checks, monthly deep dives |
| Regulatory preparedness | Design with EU AI Act requirements in mind; explainability built in |

## 10. Risk Register

### Tier 1: High Impact, High Likelihood

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation | HIO Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Identity crisis / resistance** | Team fragmentation, attrition | Address identity BEFORE structure change (Part II). Invitation, not mandate. Psychological safety | Pillar 3: Fulfillment |
| **Productivity dip during transition** | Leadership loses confidence | Set expectations (15% temporary dip OK). Show outcome metrics improving alongside | Pillar 4: Measure Whole |
| **AI tools not mature enough** | Can't achieve target depth | Start with what's available. Build incrementally. Don't block on perfect tooling | Pillar 2: Harmonize |

### Tier 2: High Impact, Medium Likelihood

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation | HIO Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Senior engineers feel devalued** | Attrition of best people | Frame clearly: "Your judgment is MORE valuable — AI makes you more powerful, not less" | Pillar 3: Fulfillment |
| **Over-reliance on AI without quality gates** | Production incidents (Amazon scenario) | Governance framework (Section 9). Human review mandatory for high-impact paths | Pillar 4: Measure |
| **6-month timeline too aggressive** | Forced adoption without readiness | Exit criteria > calendar dates. Extend phases if needed | Pillar 2: Ephemeral structure |
| **Fulfillment drops despite good metrics** | Hollow transformation | Bi-weekly fulfillment pulse. Pause and address immediately if scores drop | Pillar 3: Fulfillment |

### Tier 3: Medium Impact, High Likelihood

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation | HIO Principle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rest of org resentful** | Cultural tension | Share everything openly. Gradient adoption. Don't position as "elite team" | Pillar 2: Harmonize |
| **Metric gaming** | False signal of success | Multiple metric layers make gaming harder. Qualitative check alongside quantitative | Pillar 4: Measure |
| **AI Productivity Paradox** | Individual gains don't become org gains | Focus on OUTCOMES not output. Redesign workflows, not just add AI to existing ones | Pillar 1: Purpose |

### Learning from Industry Failures

| Company | What Failed | How We Avoid It |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon (Kiro mandate) | Top-down mandate without quality safeguards | Voluntary participation, governance framework, no usage percentage targets |
| Klarna (full replacement) | Replacing humans entirely with AI | Hybrid model always — AI absorbs repetitive work, humans retain judgment/creative/relational |
| Meta (75% AI code target) | Measuring AI code percentage instead of outcomes | We measure outcomes (time-to-experiment, downstream NPS) not AI usage percentages |
| Industry-wide (95% pilot failure) | Pilots that don't scale | Phase gates with exit criteria. Prove at each phase before expanding |
| DORA limitations | Metrics can't capture AI impact | Three-layer model with AI Attribution awareness |
